# Job Search, Applications and Interviews

## The job search funnel
1. **Target** — define role, industry, location and level.
2. **Source** — job boards, company career pages, referrals, recruiters, communities.
3. **Apply** — tailored CV and cover letter.
4. **Screen** — recruiter call and take-home or assessment.
5. **Interview loop** — technical and behavioural rounds.
6. **Offer and negotiation**.

## Referrals beat cold applications
Referrals dramatically improve response rates. Build a genuine network: alumni, communities,
open-source collaborators, conferences, and informational interviews. A referral is usually
a warm internal advocate, not a favour from a stranger.

## Writing a CV
- One page early career; two at most.
- Lead with **impact and evidence**: action + what you did + result (with numbers when
  possible).
- Tailor keywords to each posting (many companies use applicant tracking systems).
- Order: contact, summary (optional), experience, projects, skills, education.
- Quantify: "reduced load time 40%", "grew users from 0 to 1,200", "cut costs 15%".

## Tailoring applications
For each role, mirror the language of the posting. Address the top 3-5 requirements
explicitly. A generic CV optimised for everything is competitive for nothing.

## Portfolio and proof of work
In tech and many adjacent fields, a portfolio (GitHub, personal site, case studies,
write-ups) is often more persuasive than a CV claim. Make the value obvious in 30 seconds:
what problem, what you built, what the result was, how to run it.

## Interview types
- **Behavioural:** STAR stories on collaboration, conflict, ownership, failure.
- **Technical:** coding, SQL, case, modelling, or a domain exercise.
- **Case:** structured problem-solving, often consulting or product.
- **Take-home:** a small project assessed for clarity, correctness and communication.
- **System design:** for mid/senior engineering and architecture roles.

## Preparing with the STAR method
Structure answers as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Prepare 6-8 flexible stories
(leadership, conflict, failure, ambiguity, technical challenge, teamwork, initiative,
prioritisation) and re-angle them per question. Always end on the result and a lesson.

## Low-latency practice that works
- Mock interviews with peers or recorded solo sessions.
- Rehearse your "tell me about yourself" to 60-90 seconds.
- Prepare 2-3 thoughtful questions for the interviewer.
- Practice explaining a recent project out loud at three levels of detail.

## After the interview
Send a brief thank-you note. Reflect on gaps while fresh. If rejected, ask for feedback
where appropriate and treat it as data — rejection is usually about fit or volume, not
personal worth.

## Evaluating an offer
Weigh role scope, manager quality, learning, compensation, flexibility and stability. Ask
to speak with a future teammate if possible. Negotiate respectfully with market data.
