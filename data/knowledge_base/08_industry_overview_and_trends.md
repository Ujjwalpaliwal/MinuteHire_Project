# Industry Overview and Trends

## Why industry context matters
The same role can feel completely different depending on the industry, funding model and
company stage. Understanding industries helps you target roles where demand, pay and
learning are strongest.

## Key technology-adjacent industries
- **SaaS / B2B software:** recurring revenue; roles in product, engineering, sales, success.
- **FinTech:** payments, lending, investing, insurtech; regulated, data-heavy.
- **HealthTech / BioTech:** clinical data, compliance, long sales cycles.
- **EdTech:** learning platforms; mission-driven, price-sensitive markets.
- **E-commerce / Marketplaces:** growth, logistics, analytics heavy.
- **Gaming / Media / Entertainment:** creative plus technical; project-based.
- **AI / GenAI:** fast-moving; retrieval, agents, evaluation, model operations.
- **Cybersecurity:** persistent demand; compliance and threat driven.
- **ClimateTech / Energy:** policy-linked growth; engineering and analytics roles.
- **Public sector / GovTech:** stability, scale, slower pace.

## Company stage and what it means for you
- **Startup (1-50):** broad scope, high ownership, higher risk, equity-heavy pay.
- **Scale-up (50-500):** process emerging, strong learning, still fast-moving.
- **Large enterprise (500+):** stability, structure, specialisation, slower promotion.
- **Agency / consultancy:** variety of clients, fast exposure, service pressure.

## Structural trends shaping careers (2024-2026+)
- **Generative AI everywhere:** retrieval-augmented generation, agents, evaluation and
  context engineering are becoming baseline skills across roles.
- **Automation of routine work:** repetitive tasks are increasingly automated, raising the
  premium on judgment, communication and system design.
- **Data and privacy regulation:** GDPR-style rules create demand for compliance and
  privacy-aware engineering.
- **Remote and hybrid work:** broader geographic job access but more competition.
- **Skills-based hiring:** portfolios, projects and assessments increasingly matter
  alongside (or instead of) credentials.
- **Lifelong learning:** continuous upskilling is expected, not optional.

## How to research an industry or company
1. Read a recent industry report or a company's annual report and product updates.
2. Follow practitioners on professional networks and read their analyses.
3. Talk to 2-3 people working in the field (informational interviews).
4. Check job postings to see the real skill and experience requirements.
5. Look at funding, growth and layoffs in the news to gauge stability.

## Choosing an industry
Match industry to your values and risk tolerance:
- Want stability and mission? Public sector, healthcare, education.
- Want speed and upside? Startups, AI, fintech.
- Want variety? Agencies and consultancies.
The role you do matters most day-to-day, but the industry shapes pay, stability and exit
options. Ideally optimise for a role you like in an industry that is growing.
