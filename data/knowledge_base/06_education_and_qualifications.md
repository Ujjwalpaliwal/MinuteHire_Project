# Education, Qualifications and Alternatives

## Do you need a specific degree?
- **Software and most tech roles:** a degree helps but is not strictly required; portfolios,
  internships and demonstrated skills often matter more than the major.
- **Data science / ML research:** degrees (often a master's or PhD) matter more because of
  the mathematics and research expectations.
- **Business roles (consulting, finance):** degrees and internships matter; target-school
  recruiting exists but strong candidates from any school break through with proof of work.
- **Regulated professions** (medicine, law, accounting, engineering chartership, teaching)
  require specific qualifications and licences.

## Undergraduate vs postgraduate
- A **master's** is most worth it when: the field requires it, you are switching domains,
  you want research access, or a specific employer gate-keeps on it. It is weakest when used
  purely to delay career decisions.
- **MBA** is usually best taken after several years of work experience with a clear goal.

## Certifications: when they help
Certifications are valuable when an employer explicitly asks for them or when they build
credible, verifiable skills:
- Cloud: AWS/Azure/GCP associate certifications.
- Data: vendor analytics or BI certifications; SQL is best proven by projects.
- Project management: PMP/PRINCE2 in operations-heavy industries.
- Security: CompTIA Security+, CISSP (later career).
Certifications rarely substitute for a portfolio or experience; they complement it.

## Bootcamps and self-study
- Good for: career switchers, fast skill acquisition, structured accountability.
- Watch out for: high cost, variable quality, job-placement claims. Verify outcomes and talk
  to alumni.
- Self-study works when paired with projects, public proof and feedback loops.

## Alternative and non-linear routes
- **Apprenticeships and internships** — earn while you learn, strong conversion to full-time.
- **Freelancing and contract work** — build a portfolio and earn; can convert to permanent.
- **Open source** — visible, credible skill evidence that recruiters respect.
- **Hackathons and competitions** — fast portfolio pieces and networking.
- **Startups** — broad experience and responsibility early; higher risk.
- **Internal moves** — change roles from inside where your track record already counts.

## Choosing a course or programme: checklist
1. Does it teach the exact skills in your target job postings?
2. Is there a real project or portfolio output?
3. What do recent graduates actually do now?
4. What is the cost per outcome, including opportunity cost of time?
5. Can you get feedback from practitioners?

## International study and work
- Consider visa and work-rights constraints early; they often shape degree choices.
- Some countries gate skilled migration on specific credentials or in-demand occupations.
- English proficiency tests (IELTS/TOEFL) may be required.

## Lifelong learning expectation
Skills decay quickly in technology-heavy fields. Budget recurring time (a few hours a week)
for learning, and prefer learning tied to a real project over passive course consumption.
