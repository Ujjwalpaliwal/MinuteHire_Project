# Frequently Asked Student Scenarios

## "I don't know what I want to do."
This is normal. Narrowing starts from evidence, not introspection. Pick two or three
directions that sound plausible, then run one cheap experiment in each (a small project, a
course, an informational chat). Compare your energy and progress afterward. Treat the
decision as reversible — you are choosing the next step, not your whole life.

## "I like building products but not hardcore coding."
You are a strong fit for product management, solutions/sales engineering, developer
relations, UX/product design, technical program management, data analysis and technical
writing. Your ability to build is a differentiator in all of these. See the technology
careers document for entry routes and required skills.

## "My degree is unrelated to what I want to do."
Many people pivot. Build proof of work in the target field (projects, internships,
freelance, certifications), get feedback from practitioners, and use transferable skills
(analysis, communication, ownership) as the bridge. A short bridge programme or master's
can help for credentialed fields, but is often optional elsewhere.

## "I have no experience and no one will hire me."
Create experience: personal projects, open source, freelancing, volunteering, competitions
or internships. Choose projects that mirror real work and publish them with a clear write-up.
Simultaneously grow referrals through communities and alumni so your applications are not
cold.

## "Should I do a master's or start working?"
Work first if you can get a role that teaches you and that you would take anyway; experience
sharpens the decision about whether further study is worth it. Study if your target field
requires it, if a specific employer gates on it, or if you need to switch domains and lack
the foundation.

## "How do I know if a role is a good fit?"
Look for: daily tasks you find tolerable-to-engaging, skills you can build, people you can
learn from, and values that do not clash with yours. Tests: a shadowing day, a
conversation with someone in the role, or a small freelance task. Fit is discovered by doing,
not only by imagining.

## "I'm torn between salary and passion."
Early career: prioritise learning, skills and signal, which compound into higher earnings
later. Mid-career: balance both, and note that extreme underpaying can itself restrict your
options. Very few choices are all-or-nothing; look for roles with growth and reasonable pay.

## "I keep getting rejected."
Treat the job search as a funnel and fix the step that is failing. No responses? Tailor
applications and seek referrals. Screens but no interviews? Improve your CV and keywords.
Interviews but no offers? Practice behavioural and technical rounds with feedback. Rejection
is usually about fit and volume, not your worth.

## "I'm scared of making the wrong choice."
Most career choices are recoverable. Optimise for career capital (skills, network,
reputation) so that whatever you choose, your future options expand. Curiosity plus
persistence over time compounds more than any single "perfect" decision.
